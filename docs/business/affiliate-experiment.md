# Affiliate experiment plan: 10 Products, 30 Variants

This is the plan for the first experiment run in the Affiliate Lab. Its purpose is to learn which Products, creative templates and Hooks are worth more effort, and whether Product Lock videos earn Commission at all. It is not expected to be profitable.

Terms in capitals are defined in `CONTEXT.md`.

Nothing in this document is verified market data. Every number below is a starting assumption chosen so the experiment has rules, and each is marked **(assumption)**. Replace them with what the first weeks show.

## What we want to learn

1. Does a Product Lock video get watched and clicked at all?
2. Does the creative template change the result for the same Product?
3. Does the Hook change the result for the same creative template?
4. Which kinds of Product are worth a second round?

## The design

- **10 Products, 3 Variants each, 30 Variants in total.**
- **Products 1–5 test creative templates.** Each gets one Variant per template (Luxury Cinematic, Product Showcase, Problem–Solution). The Hook necessarily differs between templates, so these Products tell us about the template and Hook together.
- **Products 6–10 test Hooks.** Each gets three Variants in the same creative template (Product Showcase) with three different Hooks, so the Hook is the only thing that changes.
- **One Project per Product.** Its three Variants therefore share one brief: the same audience, language, target duration (20 seconds for all ten Products) and objective. Only the creative template or the Hook differs between them.
- **One Campaign per half**: "Template test" and "Hook test". Each Campaign holds its 15 Variants.
- **One Rendered Video per Variant, one Published Post per Rendered Video, all on the same account.** Publishing the same video to a second account creates a second Published Post and is left for a later round, so that the account is not a variable in this one.

### Hook types for Products 6–10

Use one of each per Product, so the three types are compared across five Products:

| Type | Shape | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Asks the viewer about a situation they know | Must not imply a claim no Fact supports |
| Statement of a Fact | Leads with the most striking Confirmed Fact | Wording stays within the Fact |
| Situation | Names a moment where the Product is used | Describes a situation, not a result |

## Choosing the 10 Products

A Product qualifies only if all of these hold:

- You can get at least three clear photos you have the right to use.
- You can write at least three Facts and Confirm each one from the seller's page or from the Product in your hands.
- It needs no claim about health, safety, certification or results to be appealing.
- The affiliate programme gives you a link for it, and ideally lets you create more than one link or tracking tag.
- The commission rate or amount is known and recorded on the Product.
- It can be understood from a still photo. Products that only make sense in motion or in use are a poor fit for Product Lock.

Use two or three categories with at least three Products in each **(assumption)**, so that every category has enough Products to compare.

Record the reason for choosing each Product in its research notes before any video is made.

## Publishing routine

For each Variant:

1. Render the Storyboard, review the Rendered Video and approve it. If any Scene was Manually Edited, re-read its text against the Product's Facts yourself, because the system no longer checks it.
2. Create an affiliate link for this Published Post alone, wherever the programme allows. A shared link means clicks and Commission can only be reported for the link or the Product, never for the video.
3. Publish by hand. Add the platform's label for AI-generated or promotional content where the platform requires one.
4. Record the Published Post straight away: platform, account, date, URL, affiliate link.

Keep the conditions the same for all 30:

- Same account.
- One Published Post per day **(assumption)**, at the same time of day.
- The three Variants of one Product are published on consecutive days, and the order of templates or Hook types is rotated from Product to Product, so no template always goes first.
- Same style of caption and the same number of hashtags.
- No paid promotion.

At one a day the publishing takes 30 days.

## What to record, and when

| What | Where it comes from | When |
|---|---|---|
| Views, likes, comments, shares | The platform's own screen for the Published Post, typed in or imported by CSV | 24 hours, 72 hours and 7 days after publishing |
| Clicks | The affiliate programme's report for the link | 7 days after publishing, then weekly |
| Orders, confirmed orders, Commission, refunds | The affiliate programme's report | Weekly, and again when the programme confirms orders |
| Production cost | Recorded by the system at render time | Automatic |

Rules for recording:

- Performance Snapshots are running totals as shown by the platform at that moment. Never enter the increase since last time.
- Leave a metric empty when you do not have it. Empty means unknown; zero means the platform showed zero.
- A wrong entry is corrected by adding a newer snapshot.
- A Performance Snapshot has one source. Figures read from the platform go in one snapshot; clicks read from the affiliate report go in a separate snapshot that names the affiliate report as its source.
- Clicks are entered against a Published Post only when its affiliate link is used by no other Published Post. Clicks on a shared link are not entered per Published Post at all.
- Record Commission at the level the report gives it. If two Published Posts share a link, the Commission belongs to the link.

The 7-day snapshot is the one used for comparison, so that every Published Post has had the same time to be seen.

## Reviewing the results

Review once a week while posting, and once in full 7 days after the last post.

At each weekly review, look only for problems with the process: Published Posts not recorded, snapshots missed, links shared by mistake, videos Flagged for Review. Do not change the plan because one video did well or badly.

At the final review:

1. Compare creative templates using Products 1–5, looking at each Product's three Variants side by side. Each template came with its own Hook, so what is compared is the template together with its Hook.
2. Compare Hook types using Products 6–10 the same way.
3. Compare Products using each Product's three Published Posts together.
4. Compare Commission and estimated profit per Product.

### What counts as enough to act on

Thirty Published Posts is a small experiment, and one per Variant means a single lucky or unlucky video can dominate. This round cannot name a winner. The most it can do is point at what deserves a second, larger round. So:

- **Small differences are ignored.** Within one Product, a Variant leads only if it has at least double the 7-day views of both others **(assumption)**. Otherwise that Product has no leader. The assumption behind the threshold is that near-identical videos can differ a great deal in views by chance; the first weeks will show whether double is too strict or too loose.
- **A pattern is worth retesting only if it repeats.** A template or Hook type is worth a second round if it leads, by the rule above, for at least 4 of its 5 Products **(assumption)**. With three options and five Products, the same option would come top 4 times out of 5 by pure chance roughly one time in seven, so even this is a reason to test again and not a result.
- **Rates need volume.** Click-through rate is only read for Published Posts with at least 1,000 views **(assumption)** and their own affiliate link. Conversion rate is not compared between Variants at all in this round, because order counts will be too low.
- **Commission is read per Product, not per video,** unless every Published Post of that Product had its own link.
- **"No clear difference" is a valid result.** It means the next round should change something bigger.

What this experiment cannot tell us:

- Whether a result would hold on another account, another platform or another month.
- Why a video did well. Posting time, the platform's distribution and chance are all mixed in.
- Whether a creative template works apart from the Hook it was paired with.
- Which video caused an order, when Published Posts share a link or when a buyer saw more than one of them.
- Anything about generative Techniques, since every video is Product Lock.

## After the experiment

Decide one of three things and write down why:

- **Retest what repeated.** Take the template, Hook type or category that met the rule above and run a second round with more Variants of it.
- **Change the look.** If views were uniformly low across all 30, the format is the likely problem, and the next investment is in how the videos look rather than in more Products.
- **Change the Products.** If views were reasonable but clicks and Commission were not, the Product choice or the offer is the likely problem.
